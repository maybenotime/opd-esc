TL;DROn-policy distillation teaches a student where to branch, not only how to branch. We propose Entropy Shape Correlation (ESC) to measure student–teacher agreement on where to branch, showing that this agreement causally drives OPD gains and can be used before training to select the best teacher.

Motivation

A stronger teacher does not guarantee a better student. When distilling R1-Distill-1.5B, a weaker teacher post-trained with RL beats a stronger teacher trained with the student's own recipe.

Prior work credits the gap to new knowledge the teacher gains in post-training (Li et al., 2026). However, it remains unclear what this knowledge is, and such analyses only diagnose OPD after training. This leaves two open questions:

Q1What does the student learn from the teacher in OPD? Q2Can we predict a teacher's gain before training?

What OPD learns: where to branch and how to branch

RL post-training shapes reasoning mainly through a small set of high-entropy fork tokens. We hypothesize that OPD transfers how the teacher behaves at those forks. That behavior has two parts, and each needs its own measurement. We identify where to branch as a key component of what OPD learns, characterize it with the entropy shape, and propose ESC to measure student-teacher alignment in entropy shape.

Where to branch: Entropy shape

Entropy Shape Correlation (ESC) is the Pearson correlation between the student's and teacher's entropy sequences along the same trajectory. It excludes the effect of differences in absolute entropy levels and measures the alignment of entropy shape alone.

$$\rho = \mathrm{Pearson}\big(\{H^p_t\}_{t=1}^T,\ \{H^q_t\}_{t=1}^T\big)$$
ESC · new in this work

How to branch: Token candidates

Overlap@$k$ is the average fraction of tokens shared by the student's and teacher's top-$k$ sets at each position. It measures how well the student and teacher agree on how to branch.

$$\mathrm{Overlap@}k = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{|S^{(p)}_t \cap S^{(q)}_t|}{k}$$
Overlap@16 · Li et al., 2026

Notation. Let $p_t$ and $q_t$ denote the student's and teacher's next-token distributions at position $t \in \{1, \dots, T\}$ of the student's own rollout. Let $H^p_t$ and $H^q_t$ be their entropies, and let $S^{(p)}_t = \mathrm{TopK}(p_t, k)$ and $S^{(q)}_t = \mathrm{TopK}(q_t, k)$ be their top-$k$ token sets.

Finding 1OPD does not rely on correct supervision

Is the teacher's extra knowledge just its ability to solve more training problems? We build two 7k-prompt subsets from DeepMath-103K: both-correct (both teachers solve every prompt) and both-wrong (both fail). Each student is paired with a same-recipe teacher and an RL post-trained teacher.

The post-trained teacher still helps when it solves none of the training problems, and the same-recipe teacher still fails when it solves all of them. This suggests that OPD transfers a general reasoning behavior rather than answers.

Finding 2Successful OPD aligns the entropy shape

Before OPD, the student's entropy rises and falls out of step with the teacher's. After successful OPD training, their entropy profiles become nearly identical: for the same prompt, ESC increases from 0.85 to 0.99.

Throughout training, ESC clearly separates successful runs from unsuccessful ones. In successful runs, ESC rises substantially alongside the student's average benchmark performance, and both level off once ESC saturates. In unsuccessful runs, ESC starts high and plateaus quickly. Overlap@16 shows a similar pattern. In contrast, the entropy gap $\Delta H$, the absolute difference between the student's and teacher's mean per-position entropies, converges to similarly low values in every run and fails to tell the two apart, because it loses the positional information of entropy along the trajectory.

Finding 3Where-to-branch alignment causally affects OPD gains

The alignment above is correlational. To isolate the causal role of the entropy shape, we construct counterfactual teachers by transplanting one teacher's entropy shape onto another. Let $z_{A,t}$ be the logits of teacher $A$ at position $t$ of a student rollout, and $H_{B,t}$ the entropy of teacher $B$ at the same position. We denote transplanting $B$'s entropy shape onto $A$ by $A \leftarrow B$. At each position, we solve for a temperature $T_t$ and rescale $A$'s logits to obtain the counterfactual teacher $\tilde q^{\,A\leftarrow B}_t$:

$$H\big(\mathrm{softmax}(z_{A,t}/T_t)\big) = H_{B,t}, \qquad \tilde q^{\,A\leftarrow B}_t = \mathrm{softmax}(z_{A,t}/T_t)$$

Since dividing logits by a positive scalar preserves their ranking, $\tilde q^{\,A\leftarrow B}_t$ keeps $A$'s token preferences, including its argmax and top-$k$ sets, while matching $B$'s entropy at every position. Let $A$ be a successful teacher and $B$ a failing one.

  • $\tilde q^{\,B\leftarrow A}$ transplants the successful teacher's entropy shape onto the failing one.
  • $\tilde q^{\,A\leftarrow B}$ transplants the failing teacher's entropy shape onto the successful one.

How to predict OPD outcomes: pre-OPD ESC selects the best teacher

ESC can be computed before OPD starts (pre-OPD ESC) to predict which student–teacher pairs will gain the most from OPD, and to select an appropriate teacher for a given student. We formulate this as a teacher selection task: given a student and a pool of candidate teachers, each predictor scores every pair on a small probe set before training and selects the top-ranked teacher.

Evaluation metrics

Ranking
Spearman $r_s$

Rank correlation between the predictor's ranking of the candidate teachers and their ranking by realized gain $\Delta$, regardless of direction.

Best: $|r_s| = 1$
Selection
Regret

$\max_T \Delta(T) - \Delta(\hat T)$, where $\hat T$ is the predictor's top-ranked teacher: the gain lost by not choosing the best teacher in the pool.

Best: $0$
Stability
95% bootstrap CI

Interval for the signed $r_s$ from 5,000 bootstrap resamples of the ten pairings. If it contains zero, the direction of the association is not stable.
